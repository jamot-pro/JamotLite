# A good charter

A charter is what every agent and every person in the company reads before
acting. It must be short, concrete and the founder's own.

- **Vision — why it exists.** One sentence about the world the company
  builds toward, not about the company. "Nobody in the neighbourhood eats
  factory bread." Not "To be the best bakery".
- **Mission — what it does, for whom, how.** One or two sentences a
  stranger understands. Names the people it serves.
- **Goals — the next three months.** One to three. Each has a number and a
  date, or a clear yes/no: "100 regular customers by 31 December". Turn
  "grow a lot" into a number by asking, never by guessing.
- **Values — rules it never breaks.** Written as rules an agent can follow
  and a person can check: "Never sell yesterday's bread as fresh", "Never
  share a customer's details". Not adjectives ("honesty", "quality"): ask
  what honesty forbids in practice.
- **People — honest about who is there.** Only people the founder named.
  Roles nobody has yet are open, for an invitation.
- **What the founder won't do goes to agents first** when an agent can do
  it — answering, drafting, reminding, research. Money, contracts, hiring
  and physical work stay with people.
- **A business that already runs keeps what works.** Ask what it does today,
  for whom, and who already does what; the charter describes that business,
  not a new one, and goals start from where it is.
- **Small beats complete.** Three good goals and three real rules are better
  than ten of each.
