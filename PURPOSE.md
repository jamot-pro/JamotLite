# Jamot's Purpose

**Draft v0.2 · 2026-10-02 · not yet reviewed by legal counsel · private to the stewards**

> **Read this first.** This document describes how Jamot intends to be owned,
> governed and shared. It is **not an offer** of shares, securities or tokens,
> and nothing here creates a right to any. Contribution credits have no monetary
> value until they are converted under an ownership plan approved by legal
> counsel. The numbers below are the founder's commitment of intent; the legal
> instruments that make them real are being drafted. This file must not be
> published or promoted until that review is done.

How Jamot works day to day — roles, the weekly rhythm, your first step — is in
[STEWARDS.md](STEWARDS.md). This file is why Jamot exists and who owns it.

---

## 1. Vision and mission

**Vision:** a world where any group of people can start a company that outlives
any one of them.

**Mission:** build the open-source runtime that keeps small companies alive —
run by humans and agents together, owned by the people who build it.

Jamot itself is the first company built this way. Its charter — the vision,
the mission, the values it never breaks and the goals it measures — is written
in [`jamot.company.yaml`](jamot.company.yaml), the same file format any company
on Jamot uses, and it runs on Jamot Lite. Jamot keeps going until the vision is
real, and it is designed so that no single person, including its founder, is
required for it to keep going.

## 2. Why Jamot is steward-owned

Most companies can be sold, and their mission goes with the buyer. Jamot
can't.

Jamot is **steward-owned**:

1. **The people who work on it govern it.** Voting belongs to active stewards,
   one steward one vote. You can't buy votes, and you can't keep them after you
   stop contributing.
2. **Its value belongs mostly to the people who build it.** Contributors earn
   the largest share of Jamot's value, and it grows over time as investors are
   paid back.
3. **No one can sell it off its mission.** An independent guardian holds a veto
   on selling the company, changing its mission, or closing its open-source
   core.

## 3. Who owns what

Jamot separates **value** (who shares in profits and in any sale) from
**voice** (who decides).

**Value — economic rights, fully diluted:**

| Holder | Share | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Contributors** | **45%** | Earned through verified contribution, released over time (§6) |
| **Founder** | **30%** | Vests over 4 years (§8) |
| **Investors** | **25% at most** | Returns capped at 5× — then their shares return to contributors (§7) |

"Fully diluted" means the contributor pool counts from day one, even before it
is earned. Nobody's share grows because the pool is waiting.

**Voice — governance:**

| Who | Power |
|---|---|
| **Active stewards** | One steward, one vote, on strategy, budgets, allocations and the steward council |
| **Steward council** | Runs day-to-day governance and approves allocations |
| **Guardian** | Veto only, on the few things that can never happen (§10) |
| **Investors** | No vote; protections written into their agreement |

## 4. Stewards

Jamot has three roles, the same three every company on Jamot can have:

| Role | Who | Votes? |
|---|---|---|
| **Stewards** | The people responsible for Jamot: each owns a responsibility and is the successor on another | Yes, one each |
| **Backers** | The people who fund Jamot — the investors in §7 | No |
| **Taskers** | The people who pick up the tasks Jamot's agents hand out, and get credit for them | No, until they become stewards |

Stewards and taskers are both **contributors**: anyone whose contribution was
accepted shares in the contributor pool (§6).

A **steward** owns part of Jamot: a responsibility, and the heartbeats that keep
it alive.

**Joining.** While Jamot is private, joining is by invitation. Pick a
responsibility in [STEWARDS.md](STEWARDS.md), take its first step, and bring
your own agent — Claude Code, Cursor, Hermes, Codex, anything. Your first
accepted contribution makes you a contributor. Owning a responsibility for 3
months makes you a steward.

**Being active.** A steward is active while they own at least one
responsibility, or have had contribution accepted in the last 6 months.

**Leaving.** You keep everything you earned (§6). You return your steward vote,
because you can't govern a company you no longer work on. You can come back at
any time.

**The first stewards are volunteers.** They join for the mission. Their work is
recorded from day one, and the plan is for it to become ownership (§11).

## 5. How contribution is recorded

Jamot runs on Jamot. Its own runtime records who owned what, what was
delivered, and what was kept alive — the same way it will for every company
built on it.

1. **Outcomes, not activity.** Credit comes from merged work packages,
   responsibilities kept healthy, skills approved, people helped, problems
   solved — not from lines of code, messages or hours.
2. **Keeping things alive counts as much as building them.** Owning a
   responsibility whose heartbeats stay green earns steady credit.
3. **Your agent's work is your work.** If your AI wrote it, you get the
   credit — and the responsibility for it.
4. **The agent proposes, people decide.** Every quarter, the Jamot agent drafts
   an allocation from the recorded ledger, with the reasoning for each line.
   Stewards review it, anyone can contest their own line, and the steward
   council approves it.
5. **Caps.** No one receives more than a set share of a quarter's allocation,
   so influence can't be bought with volume.
6. **Visible and permanent.** The contribution ledger,
   [CONTRIBUTIONS.md](CONTRIBUTIONS.md), is visible to every steward and is
   never rewritten — only added to, and corrected by a new line. It stays
   private and internal while Jamot is private.
7. **Founding work is recorded first.** The work done before this document is
   entered at the start of the ledger, in the open, so everyone knows the
   starting point.

## 6. How the contributor pool is released

The 45% pool must last as long as Jamot does, so it is released slowly and
never runs out:

- **10% of the pool is a permanent reserve** for contributors who haven't
  arrived yet.
- **Each year, at most 20% of what remains** of the rest is allocated,
  in quarterly rounds. More is released early, when the risk is highest; less
  later; something is always left.

What that means as a share of the whole company (fully diluted):

| Year | Allocated that year | Allocated in total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 8.1% | 8.1% |
| 2 | 6.5% | 14.6% |
| 3 | 5.2% | 19.8% |
| 4 | 4.1% | 23.9% |
| 5 | 3.3% | 27.2% |

**Cliff.** A new contributor's first 3 months are recorded but vest only once
they pass the 3-month mark. After that, what you're allocated each quarter is
yours.

## 7. Investors

- Investors hold **at most 25%** of Jamot's value, across every funding round.
  New rounds come out of that 25%; they never dilute the founder or the
  contributor pool.
- Their return is **capped at 5×** what they put in.
- When an investor reaches the cap, their shares are **bought back and
  cancelled, and the same amount is added to the contributor pool.** Over time,
  Jamot returns to the people who build it.
- Investors don't vote. They are backing a steward-owned company, and they know
  it when they invest.

## 8. The founder

- The founder holds **30%**, vesting over **4 years**. If the founder leaves
  early, what hasn't vested returns to the contributor pool.
- As a steward, the founder has **one vote**, like everyone else.
- *Proposal, to confirm:* while Jamot is fragile, the founder holds a seat on the
  guardian until Jamot Lite v1.0 ships or two years pass, whichever comes first.
  It ends automatically.

## 9. Where money goes

When Jamot earns money — from Hosted runtimes, Pro and the Network — it goes, in
this order:

1. **Keeping Jamot alive and growing**: the team, the infrastructure, the
   runway.
2. **Investors, up to their 5× cap.**
3. **Distributions to value holders**: contributors and the founder, in
   proportion to what they hold.

## 10. What can never happen

The guardian can block, and can only block:

- selling Jamot, or control of it, to anyone;
- changing the vision or the mission;
- closing the open-source core, or relicensing it so it can be closed;
- removing the steward-ownership principles in this document.

The guardian cannot run Jamot, take its money or choose its work.

## 11. Honest limits

- **This is a plan, not a promise of value.** Jamot may never be worth anything.
  Contribute because you believe in the vision.
- **The legal structure isn't finished.** The share classes, the guardian, the
  conversion of contribution credits into ownership and the tax treatment are
  being set up with counsel. Until then, contribution credits are a permanent
  record, not a security.
- **Where you live matters.** Tax and securities rules differ by country; what
  you can receive, and how, may depend on yours.
- **The formula will be imperfect.** That's why people review every allocation,
  and why you can contest yours.

## 12. What happens next

1. Legal counsel reviews this document and drafts the ownership plan, the share
   classes and the guardian.
2. Jamot's contribution ledger is live from day one in
   [CONTRIBUTIONS.md](CONTRIBUTIONS.md); Jamot's own runtime
   ([`jamot.company.yaml`](jamot.company.yaml)) takes it over once it runs.
3. The first stewards join, and the first quarterly allocation is proposed.
4. Contribution credits convert into ownership once the plan is approved.
5. The same model becomes a setting any company built with Jamot can choose:
   `ownership: steward`.

## Words used here

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Steward** | Someone who owns a responsibility of Jamot and keeps it alive; active stewards vote |
| **Backer** | Someone who funds Jamot; returns are capped and backing never buys a vote |
| **Tasker** | Someone who picks up tasks Jamot's agents hand out |
| **Contributor** | Anyone whose contribution was accepted, steward or tasker; contributors share in the pool |
| **Contribution ledger** | The permanent record of what each person delivered, visible to every steward |
| **Contributor pool** | The 45% of Jamot's value reserved for contributors |
| **Guardian** | An independent holder of a veto on selling Jamot or changing its mission |
| **Steward council** | The stewards who approve allocations and run governance |
| **Fully diluted** | Percentages counted as if the whole pool were already issued |
