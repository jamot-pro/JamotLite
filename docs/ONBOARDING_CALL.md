# Onboarding call — the founder's kit

Until Jamot Keeper runs the onboarding on Telegram (first issue 9,
[GitHub #11](https://github.com/jamot-pro/JamotLite/issues/11)), the founder
runs it as a 30-minute call. Same questions, same promises
([START_HERE.md](START_HERE.md) §1). Whoever takes over this responsibility
uses the same kit.

## 1. Before the call — the invite

Send this (Telegram or email), then invite them to the `jamot-pro` GitHub
organisation once they say yes:

> Hi {name} — I'd like you to be one of Jamot's first stewards. Jamot builds a
> runtime for companies that don't die when people leave, and we run Jamot
> itself that way: every responsibility has an owner and a successor, and every
> contribution is recorded.
>
> Before we talk, have a look at the vision (README) and how we work
> (STEWARDS.md) — I'll send you repository access. In our 30-minute call we'll
> get to know each other, pick the responsibility you'd own, and you'll leave
> with your first issue.
>
> One thing to know: everyone who joins is asked — optionally — for their date,
> exact time and place of birth, so we can calculate their Human Design and
> Gene Keys and work well together. It stays private, never decides anything
> about you, and you can skip it. If you're happy to share it, have your birth
> time handy.

## 2. The call — 30 minutes

| Min | What | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Why Jamot** | Tell the story in your words: companies die when people leave; the charter runs. Ask what drew them. |
| 5–10 | **You** | Name and how they like to be called · Telegram and GitHub handles · city and time zone |
| 10–15 | **Your work** | What they're good at · what they want to learn · hours a week, honestly |
| 15–22 | **Your role** | Walk the responsibilities in STEWARDS.md; agree the one they **own** and the one they're **successor** for. Open its first issue together. |
| 22–26 | **Your design** (optional) | Explain why and the promises first. If they agree: date of birth, exact time (how sure?), place of birth. Mention other tests they can add themselves. |
| 26–30 | **Next 7 days** | Their first step: run the code (START_HERE §2), ship the first issue's first pull request with a ledger line. Agree a check-in day. |

Say the promises out loud before asking for birth details: optional, private,
never decides roles, reviews or rewards, deletable any time.

## 3. Where the answers go

| Answer | Goes to | Never to |
|---|---|---|
| Name, handles, time zone, skills, hours | Their profile in Jamot's runtime once it runs (first issue 8); until then your private notes | — |
| Owned and successor responsibility | STEWARDS.md table, `jamot.company.yaml`, `.github/CODEOWNERS` — in their first pull request | — |
| Birth date, time, place; readings; test results | **Your private notes only**, until Jamot Keeper can take them into their profile — then delete the notes | The repository, GitHub issues, the ledger, email threads, group chats, any shared document (AGENTS.md rule 12) |

## 4. After the call — checklist

- [ ] GitHub: invited to `jamot-pro`, access to JamotLite
- [ ] Their responsibility's first issue assigned to them
- [ ] Added to the stewards' Telegram group
- [ ] Their check-in day in your calendar
- [ ] Birth details (if given) in your private notes, nowhere else
- [ ] When their first pull request merges: their name in STEWARDS.md, `jamot.company.yaml` and CODEOWNERS, and their first ledger line
